1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PRODUCT VISION
Setu SHG OS is India's first and most comprehensive digital operating system designed exclusively for the 8.5+ million Self-Help Groups (SHGs) across India, collectively representing over 90 million women members. The platform unifies five critical pillars — Financial Management, Commerce & Marketplace, Learning & Skill Development, Governance & Compliance, and AI-Powered Business Intelligence — into a single, offline-capable, multilingual mobile-first platform.
The core vision is to eliminate the systemic barriers that prevent India's SHG women from accessing formal financial systems, national markets, and government-backed opportunities. By digitising the SHG ecosystem end-to-end, Setu SHG OS creates a verifiable financial identity for every member, enabling formal credit access, and builds economic bridges between rural producers and urban consumers.
1.1 Vision Statement
"To empower every SHG woman in India with digital-first tools that transform grassroots entrepreneurship into scalable, credit-worthy, market-connected businesses — building a ₹5 Lakh Crore SHG economy by 2030."
1.2 Mission Statement
Provide a single unified platform that manages SHG finances, facilitates loans, opens marketplace access, delivers skills training, connects to government schemes, and uses AI to guide every member toward sustainable livelihood outcomes.

1.4 Strategic Positioning
Setu SHG OS positions itself as the "Shopify + Zoho + Coursera" for India's SHG ecosystem — while Shopify powers global merchants, Zoho powers businesses, and Coursera powers learners, Setu SHG OS powers the entire SHG economy end-to-end within a single platform specifically designed for low-literacy, low-connectivity, vernacular-language rural users.
2. PROBLEM STATEMENT
India's 8.5 million SHGs represent one of the world's most powerful grassroots economic networks. Yet, they remain largely disconnected from formal financial systems, digital markets, and government opportunities due to multiple systemic failures:
2.1 Financial Challenges
Problem Area
Current Reality & Impact
Bookkeeping
95% of SHGs maintain paper registers that are error-prone, easily lost, and cannot be audited. Leads to disputes, loss of funds, and inability to prove creditworthiness.
Loan Access
Only 18% of SHGs have accessed formal bank credit. Banks lack digitised repayment histories to assess creditworthiness of groups.
Repayment Tracking
Internal loan repayments among members are tracked manually, leading to defaults, group conflicts, and fund mismanagement.
Savings Management
Member savings pooling lacks transparency. Leaders hold disproportionate control, creating trust deficits within groups.
No Credit Score
SHGs have no standardised creditworthiness score despite years of consistent saving and internal lending history.
2.2 Market Access Challenges
Problem Area
Current Reality & Impact
Middlemen Dependency
70% of SHG products are sold through exploitative intermediaries who capture 40–60% of profit margin, severely limiting SHG income.
Limited Customer Reach
SHG products rarely transcend their village or district. No digital storefront, no national discoverability, no D2C capability.
Lack of Branding
SHG products have no packaging standards, brand identity, or quality certifications that urban buyers expect, reducing premium pricing potential.
No Inventory Systems
SHGs cannot track stock, predict demand, or manage bulk orders — limiting ability to fulfill B2B procurement opportunities from corporates and governments.
Logistics Gap
No integrated logistics solution. SHGs cannot ship nationally without complex manual arrangements with multiple courier partners.
2.3 Governance & Transparency Challenges
Group attendance is tracked on paper — no historical record, no accountability mechanism
Leadership conflicts arise from non-transparent decision-making and fund handling
Meeting minutes are handwritten and inaccessible to external stakeholders
No compliance tracking for NABARD, government, or NGO reporting requirements
No standardised onboarding process for new members
2.4 Skills & Capacity Building Challenges
Low financial literacy: most SHG members cannot read a balance sheet or understand interest calculations
Limited digital literacy: inability to use smartphones for financial transactions
No access to entrepreneurship education in regional languages
Training programs by NGOs and government are one-time, non-persistent, and not digitally accessible
2.5 Government Scheme Access Challenges
India has 200+ government schemes for SHGs at central and state levels. Over 60% of eligible SHGs never apply due to awareness gaps
Complex documentation requirements create barriers — Aadhaar, PAN, bank account linkages, caste certificates, land records
No single platform helps SHGs check eligibility, prepare documents, and track application status
Language barriers prevent non-Hindi, non-English speaking members from accessing online scheme portals

4. PRODUCT MODULES — DETAILED SPECIFICATIONS
Setu SHG OS comprises 7 tightly integrated core modules. Each module functions independently but is designed to share data, users, and workflows with all other modules through a unified API layer.

Role assignment: President, Secretary, Treasurer, Member — with permission levels

4.1.3 Savings & Ledger Management

1. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE OVERVIEW
Setu SHG OS is built on a cloud-native, microservices architecture deployed on AWS India Region (ap-south-1 Mumbai). The platform is designed for horizontal scalability from 10,000 to 10 million users with zero re-architecture. Every component is independently deployable, monitored, and scalable.
1.1 High-Level Architecture
Architecture Pattern: Mobile-First | API-First | Offline-First | Cloud-Native
Layer
Components & Technologies
Client Layer
Flutter Mobile App (Android + iOS), React Web Dashboard (NGO/Bank/Govt), Progressive Web App (PWA) for low-storage devices
API Gateway Layer
AWS API Gateway + Kong Gateway — Rate limiting, auth, routing, logging, versioning
Microservices Layer
8 domain services: User, SHG, Finance, Marketplace, Learning, AI, Notification, Analytics — each independently deployed on ECS Fargate
Message Queue Layer
Apache Kafka — Event streaming between services. Redis Queue for async jobs (notifications, reports)
Database Layer
PostgreSQL (primary transactions), MongoDB (content/logs), Redis (caching/sessions), ElasticSearch (search)
Storage Layer
AWS S3 (files/media), CloudFront CDN (static assets, video streaming)
AI/ML Layer
OpenAI API + Google Gemini, Custom Recommendation Engine on SageMaker, Vector DB (Pinecone) for RAG
Infrastructure Layer
Terraform IaC, AWS ECS Fargate (containers), AWS RDS (managed PostgreSQL), Auto Scaling Groups
Observability Layer
Datadog APM, AWS CloudWatch, Sentry (error tracking), Grafana dashboards
1.2 Architecture Principles
Offline-First: All critical user flows (attendance, savings recording, loan entry) persist to local SQLite and sync via background service when connectivity resumes
API-First: Every feature exposed as REST + GraphQL API — enabling third-party bank, NGO, and government integrations without UI dependency
Event-Driven: Services communicate via Kafka events — loose coupling, eventual consistency, full audit trail
Data Sovereignty: All user data stored in AWS ap-south-1 (Mumbai) — RBI data localisation compliance
Zero-Trust Security: Every service-to-service call authenticated with JWT + mutual TLS — no implicit trust

Models

2.1.3 Offline-First Architecture
The mobile app uses a local-first approach where all user actions write to SQLite (via Drift) first, then sync to the server in the background. This ensures the app works in areas with zero connectivity — critical for rural India.
Local SQLite schema mirrors backend PostgreSQL — key tables replicated locally
Optimistic updates: UI updates instantly from local DB. Server sync happens asynchronously
Conflict resolution: Last-write-wins for non-financial data; manual review flagged for conflicting financial entries
Sync queue: All offline actions queued in Hive. Processed FIFO on next connection with retry + exponential backoff
Delta sync: Only changed records synced — not full dataset. Reduces data usage on 2G connections

APK size target: < 20MB (split APKs per ABI — arm64-v8a, armeabi-v7a)

Image compression: All uploaded images auto-compressed to < 200KB before upload

Memory management: BLoC closed when screen exits — prevents memory leaks on 1GB RAM devices

5.4 SHG Credit Scoring Engine
The SHG Credit Score (SCS) is a proprietary 0–100 score calculated for each SHG group on a weekly basis. It is the primary data product of Setu SHG OS, enabling formal bank lending to previously uncreditworthy groups.
Calculation frequency: Nightly batch job (2 AM IST) recalculates all active SHG scores
Real-time recalculation: Score recalculated instantly when a qualifying event occurs (loan repayment, savings deposit, attendance marked)
Model: Weighted scoring with 18 parameters — weights calibrated via logistic regression on historical SHG loan repayment data
Explainability: Every score comes with factor breakdown — banks and SHGs see exactly why score is X
API: GET /finance/shg/{id}/score returns score, trend, 6-month history, and improvement recommendations

3.2 Unique Competitive Advantages
Moat 1: Data Network Effect
Every SHG that uses Setu generates proprietary financial, transactional, and behavioral data. This data improves the SHG Credit Score model, the recommendation engine, and the pricing engine. More SHGs → better data → better credit scores → more banks → more loans → more SHGs. This virtuous cycle creates a deepening data moat that cannot be replicated quickly.
Moat 2: Trust & Institutional Relationships
Setu integrates with NABARD, DAY-NRLM, state NRLMs, and commercial banks. These institutional relationships take years to build and provide regulatory credibility, distribution reach (SHG field officers as product ambassadors), and data access that a new entrant cannot achieve.
Moat 3: Offline-First + Vernacular Architecture
Our offline-first Flutter app and 12-language support took 18 months to architect correctly. The AI models are fine-tuned on Indian SHG domain data in regional languages. Competitors who try to replicate this face an 18-24 month technology gap.
Moat 4: SHG Credit Score (SCS) Proprietary Asset
The SHG Credit Score, built from platform data, is a first-of-its-kind financial product for group lending. Once banks begin underwriting against SCS, it becomes a self-reinforcing standard — similar to how CIBIL became the standard for individual credit in India.

8. RISK ANALYSIS & MITIGATION
Risk Category
Risk Description & Mitigation Strategy
Technology Risk: Connectivity
Rural India's 2G/4G coverage is patchy. Mitigation: Offline-first architecture (Flutter + SQLite). Core features function without internet. SMS fallback for critical alerts. Tested on 2G networks in UP/Bihar field conditions.
Technology Risk: Low-Literacy Users
SHG members may struggle with smartphones. Mitigation: Voice UI (Sakhi), icon-first design, field officer onboarding assistance, IVR fallback, WhatsApp-based lightweight version for non-smartphone users.
Regulatory Risk: RBI
RBI may tighten digital lending regulations. Mitigation: Loan facilitation model (not direct lending) reduces regulatory exposure. Ongoing legal counsel. Account Aggregator framework ensures compliance.
Regulatory Risk: DPDP Act
New data privacy law creates compliance obligations. Mitigation: Privacy-by-design architecture, data minimisation, consent management built from Day 1. DPO appointed in Year 1.
Market Risk: NRLM Competition
Government may build competing platform. Mitigation: Current NRLM MIS (Sangam) is 15 years old, critically under-resourced, and cannot be upgraded to a modern platform. Govt likely to partner, not compete.
Market Risk: Low Smartphone Penetration
20-30% of SHG members in deep rural may not own smartphones. Mitigation: SHG secretary acts as digital proxy — one smartphone per group for financial operations. Individual features delivered via WhatsApp + USSD (Phase 2).
Financial Risk: Loan NPA
SHGs we facilitate loans for may default, damaging bank relationships. Mitigation: SHG Credit Score vets quality before application. We only facilitate, not guarantee. NPA risk carried by bank.
Operational Risk: Field Team Quality
Poor field implementation destroys trust. Mitigation: 2-week intensive training program for field officers. Regional language materials. 30-day handholding for each new SHG onboarded.
Competitive Risk: Big Tech Entry
Google, Meta, or NPCI may build competing tools. Mitigation: Data moat + institutional relationships are our primary defence. Platform SHG Credit Score standard is irreplicable quickly. First-mover advantage in government partnerships.
